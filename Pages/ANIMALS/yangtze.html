Yangtze FINLESS PORPOISE
The Yangtze Finless Porpoise is the odd one out of its family as it is the only living freshwater porpoise found in the world.[13] This aquatic mammal currently resides in the Yangtze River in China and is listed as a critically endangered species. While the Yangtze river plays a crucial role in healthy ecosystem functioning, years of environmental degradation, overfishing and water pollution in the region had detrimental impacts on many animal species who call it their home. Once upon a time, Yangtze river dolphins used to live alongside finless porpoises, but there haven't been any freshwater dolphin sightings since 2002.[14] Sadly, this could be yet another stark reminder of what soon may follow for many endangered species, including the Yangtze finless porpoise. To protect this species, China has upgraded finless porpoises to ‘first level protected species’ in 2021—the highest level of protection available in the country and issued a 10 year fishing ban over the full length of the river. Results from the 2022 census conducted by the Chinese Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs show the first increase in Yangtze finless porpoise numbers since records began - rising from 1,012 to 1,249 over five years which is a great conservation success.[15]
