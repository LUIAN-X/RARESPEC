Spiny Babbler

Scientific Name:

Turdoides nipalensis

Description:

Spiny Babbler is the only bird species found exclusively in Nepal, making it a unique symbol of the country's biodiversity. It inhabits dry scrublands and bushes in centralNepal,and its conservation is important for protecting Nepal's endemic wildlife.

Himalayan Monal

Scientific Name:

Lophophorus impejanus

Description:

Himalayan Monal is Nepal's national bird, famous for its brilliant rainbow-colored feathers and striking appearance. Found in the Himalayan forests at high altitudes, it plays an important role in maintaining a healthy mountain ecosystem and is protected due to habitat loss and hunting.

Giant Hornbill

Scientific Name:

Buceros bicornis

Description:

Giant Hornbill, is one of the world's largest hornbill species, recognized by its massive yellow casque and impressive wingspan. Found in the forests of southern Nepal, it plays a vital role in seed dispersal, helping maintain healthy forest ecosystems, but is threatened by habitat loss and hunting.

Lesser Florican

Scientific Name:

Sypheotides indicus

Description:

Lesser Florican is one of the world's smallest bustards, famous for the male's spectacular vertical jumps during the breeding season. It inhabits grasslands and is classified as Critically Endangered, with habitat loss posing the greatest threat to its survival.

Bengal Florican

Scientific Name:

Houbaropsis bengalensis

Description:

Bengal Florican is a Critically Endangered grassland bird found in the lowland grasslands of Nepal, especially in protected areas such as Chitwan and Koshi Tappu. It is one of the world's rarest bustards, and conserving its habitat is essential for its survival.

Cheer Pheasant

Scientific Name:

Catreus wallichii

Description:

Cheer Pheasant is a rare Himalayan pheasant found on steep grassy slopes and open forests of western Nepal. It is classified as Vulnerable, with habitat degradation and human disturbance posing major threats to its survival.

🕊️ How Can We Protect Birds?

Birds are an important part of our ecosystem. They help control insects, spread seeds, pollinate plants, and maintain the balance of nature. Unfortunately, many bird species are threatened due to deforestation, pollution, climate change, hunting, and illegal wildlife trade. We all can help protect birds by taking simple actions in our daily lives.

✔ Protect forests, wetlands, and natural habitats.

✔ Never hunt birds or destroy their nests and eggs.

✔ Reduce plastic use and keep the environment clean.

✔ Plant native trees and flowering plants for food and shelter.

✔ Do not buy or sell wild birds as pets.

✔ Report illegal wildlife trade to the concerned authorities.

✔ Spread awareness among your family, friends, and community.

"Protect Birds Today, Preserve Nature for Tomorrow."
